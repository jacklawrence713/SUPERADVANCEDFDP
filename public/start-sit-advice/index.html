How to Use Start Sit Rankings Effectively

The key to start sit decisions is understanding floor versus ceiling. In a matchup where you are the underdog and need a big week, lean toward high-ceiling plays — the deep-threat receiver, the backup running back in a potential shootout, the quarterback facing a bottom-five secondary. When you are favored and just need a solid outing, prioritize floor — the workhorse back, the target hog in PPR, the reliable tight end who catches six passes every week. Our rankings reflect both dimensions so you can tailor your lineup strategy to your weekly situation.

Matchup-Based Recommendations

Not all matchups are created equal. A running back facing the league's top rush defense might be a sit even if he is a top-15 player overall, while a mid-tier receiver going against a depleted secondary could be a smash start.
