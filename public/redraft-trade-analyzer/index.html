Redraft Trade Analyzer 2026 — Win Your Seasonal League Trades

Not every fantasy football league is a dynasty league. Millions of managers play in redraft leagues where rosters reset every year, and the only thing that matters is this season's production. If you are in a redraft league, you need a trade analyzer that evaluates players based on their 2026 projected output — not their long-term dynasty value. A 22-year-old rookie sitting behind a veteran starter might be worth a fortune in dynasty but almost nothing in redraft. Fantasy Draft Pros handles both formats, and our redraft trade analyzer is built specifically for seasonal leagues.

The redraft trade analyzer uses current-season projected points as the foundation for every player's value. We factor in scoring format — PPR, half-PPR, or standard — because the same player can have vastly different trade values depending on how receptions are scored. A running back who catches 60 passes is far more valuable in PPR than in standard. A touchdown-dependent tight end gains relative value in standard where his receptions are not rewarded. Toggle your scoring format in the analyzer and watch the trade values shift accordingly.

Redraft vs. Dynasty: Different Values, Different Decisions

The biggest mistake redraft managers make when using trade calculators is relying on dynasty values. In dynasty, a player like a 23-year-old receiver with moderate production but elite draft capital carries significant value because of his multi-year upside. In redraft, that same player might be a bench stash with minimal trade value because he is not producing right now. Conversely, a 31-year-old running back on a great offense might be a top-10 redraft asset despite being nearly worthless in dynasty due to his age. Our redraft analyzer strips away the dynasty premium and focuses purely on what each player is expected to produce this season.

When to Trade in Redraft Leagues

Timing matters enormously in redraft trades. Early in the season, you are trading on projections and perceived value. As the season progresses, you are trading on actual performance and remaining schedule. A player who has been disappointing through Week 4 might still have strong trade value if his schedule gets easier in the second half. A hot start from a journeyman running back might be a sell-high opportunity before regression hits. Fantasy Draft Pros updates redraft values weekly throughout the season so your trade analysis always reflects the latest data. Use it before every trade negotiation to make sure you are getting fair value — or better.
