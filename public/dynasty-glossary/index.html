Dynasty Fantasy Football Glossary — Terms and Definitions

Dynasty fantasy football has its own language. Whether you are a first-time dynasty player or a seasoned veteran, understanding the terminology is essential for navigating trades, drafts, and league management. This comprehensive glossary covers every major term you will encounter in dynasty fantasy football leagues. Bookmark this page and reference it whenever you come across an unfamiliar term.

League Format Terms

Dynasty League

A fantasy football league where managers keep their entire roster from year to year, with no annual redraft. Dynasty leagues emphasize long-term roster building, making player age and future potential critical factors in trade and draft decisions.

Keeper League

A hybrid format where managers keep a limited number of players (typically 2-5) from the previous season while the remaining roster is redrafted. Keeper leagues offer some dynasty-style strategy without the full commitment of maintaining an entire roster year over year.

Redraft League

A traditional fantasy football league where all players are drafted fresh each season. No players carry over. Redraft leagues focus entirely on current-season production rather than long-term value.

Superflex (SF)

A league format that includes a Superflex roster spot where managers can start any offensive player, including a quarterback. In practice, nearly every manager starts a second QB in this slot, making quarterback values significantly higher than in standard one-QB leagues.

Two-QB (2QB)

A league format that requires managers to start two quarterbacks each week. Similar to Superflex but with a mandatory second QB slot rather than an optional flex. Quarterback scarcity is even more extreme in 2QB leagues.

TEP (Tight End Premium)

A scoring setting that awards tight ends extra points per reception, typically an additional 0.5 to 1.0 points on top of the standard PPR scoring. TEP leagues increase the value of elite receiving tight ends and create a stronger positional premium.

PPR (Points Per Reception)

A scoring format that awards one point for every reception a player records. PPR scoring increases the value of pass-catching running backs and high-volume wide receivers compared to standard scoring.

Half-PPR

A scoring format that awards 0.5 points per reception. Half-PPR is considered a middle ground between standard and full PPR, balancing the value of volume receivers with efficient scorers.

IDP (Individual Defensive Player)

A league format that requires managers to roster and start individual defensive players such as linebackers, defensive linemen, safeties, and cornerbacks, rather than using a team defense. IDP leagues add significant roster management depth and strategic complexity.

Roster and Draft Terms

Taxi Squad

A special roster designation in dynasty leagues for rookie or young players who are being developed. Taxi squad players do not count against your active roster limit but typically cannot be started in your lineup. Most leagues allow players to remain on the taxi squad for one to three seasons before they must be promoted or released.

IR (Injured Reserve)

A roster slot designated for players who are injured and unable to play. IR slots allow managers to hold injured players without them counting against the active roster limit, freeing up space for replacement players.

Rookie Draft

An annual draft in dynasty leagues where only NFL rookies and first-year players are eligible to be selected. Rookie drafts typically occur after the NFL Draft and are a primary method of roster building in dynasty leagues.

Startup Draft

The initial draft when a new dynasty league is formed. Every NFL player is eligible, and the draft is typically much longer than a redraft (often 25-30+ rounds) since managers are building their entire dynasty roster from scratch.

Devy (Developmental)

A dynasty league variant where managers can draft college players before they enter the NFL. Devy leagues add another layer of scouting and long-term strategy, requiring managers to evaluate players one to three years before they become NFL rookies.

Draft Capital

Refers to future rookie draft picks that a manager owns. Draft capital is a valuable trade commodity in dynasty leagues. A team in rebuild mode will often trade veteran players for future draft picks to accumulate draft capital.

Strategy and Value Terms

FAAB (Free Agent Acquisition Budget)

A blind bidding system for waiver wire pickups where each manager has a fixed budget (typically $100) to bid on free agents throughout the season. The highest bid wins the player. FAAB replaces traditional waiver priority systems and rewards managers who accurately value available players.

Trade Value

A numerical representation of a player's worth in the trade market. Trade values account for current production, age, position, contract status, and league format. Fantasy Draft Pros assigns trade values to over 1,500 players to help managers evaluate trades accurately.

Buy Low

A strategy where a manager targets a player whose trade value is temporarily depressed due to injury, poor recent performance, or negative news. The goal is to acquire the player at a discount and profit when their value rebounds.

Sell High

The opposite of buy low. A manager trades away a player whose value is inflated due to a hot streak, favorable schedule, or hype. The goal is to extract maximum trade value before regression occurs.

Rebuild / Rebuilding

A dynasty team strategy focused on trading away aging veterans and current-season production for younger players and future draft picks. Rebuilding teams sacrifice short-term competitiveness to build a roster that will contend in future seasons.

Contender / Competing

A dynasty team in a position to compete for the championship in the current season. Contending teams typically trade future assets for proven veterans who can contribute immediately.

Window

The period during which a dynasty team is competitive enough to realistically win the championship. A team's window might span two to four seasons depending on roster composition and the age of core players.

Positional Scarcity

The concept that positions with fewer elite options (like tight end and quarterback) carry increased value because replacement-level production at those positions is significantly lower than at running back or wide receiver.

Age Curve

The expected production trajectory of a player based on their age and position. Running backs typically peak earlier (ages 23-26) while wide receivers and quarterbacks tend to have longer productive careers. Age curves heavily influence dynasty trade values.

Orphan Team

A dynasty roster that has been abandoned by its original manager and needs to be taken over by a new owner. Orphan teams are common in long-running dynasty leagues and can be attractive entry points for new dynasty players if the roster has been well-managed.
