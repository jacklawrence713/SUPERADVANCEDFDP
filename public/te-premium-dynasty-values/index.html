TE Premium Dynasty Values 2026 — How TEP Changes the Game

Tight end premium leagues have become one of the most popular dynasty formats, and for good reason. By awarding an extra point (or more) per tight end reception, TEP scoring transforms the tight end position from an afterthought into a legitimate difference-maker. In standard scoring, the gap between the TE1 and TE12 is relatively small. In TEP, elite tight ends who catch 80-plus passes per season become positional cheat codes worth as much as top-tier wide receivers. If you play in a TEP dynasty league, you need trade values that reflect this scoring advantage — and that is exactly what Fantasy Draft Pros provides.

Our trade calculator includes a TEP toggle that adjusts tight end values based on the premium scoring boost. When TEP is enabled, high-volume receiving tight ends see their dynasty values climb significantly, while blocking-oriented tight ends who rarely catch passes remain largely unaffected. This distinction matters enormously when evaluating trades. Swapping a mid-tier wide receiver for a top-5 tight end might look like an overpay in standard scoring, but in TEP it can be a league-winning move.

TEP Strategy: When to Pay Up for a Tight End

The key strategic insight in TEP leagues is that elite tight ends provide a positional advantage that is nearly impossible to replicate through other roster moves. There are only a handful of tight ends who consistently produce WR1-level numbers with the TEP bonus, and every team in your league wants them. This scarcity means you should be willing to pay a premium in trades to acquire a top-5 TEP tight end, especially if you are building a contending roster. On the flip side, if you are rebuilding and own one of these elite tight ends, their trade value is at its peak — you can extract significant draft capital or young player packages by selling at the right time.
