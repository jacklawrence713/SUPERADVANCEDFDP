How to Evaluate a Fantasy Football Trade

Start by identifying the total value on each side of the trade. Enter all players and draft picks involved into the Fantasy Draft Pros trade analyzer. The tool will calculate the combined value for each side and tell you which team wins the deal and by how much. A small value difference of five percent or less generally means the trade is fair and worth accepting if it addresses a roster need. A large gap of fifteen percent or more should give you pause unless there are compelling roster-specific reasons to overpay for a particular position.

Beyond raw value, consider positional need. Trading away a top-ten wide receiver hurts less if you have four other strong receivers on your roster. Acquiring a running back at a slight overpay can be worth it if your RB room is thin and you are competing for a championship. The best trades are ones where both sides improve their rosters even if the raw trade value is not perfectly equal. Use the analyzer as your starting point, then layer in your roster context to make the final decision.

When to Accept a Trade

Accept a trade when the value is close to even and the deal improves your roster at a position of need. Accept when you are selling a player at peak value who you believe will decline. Accept when you are a contender acquiring a proven asset that puts you over the top for a championship run. Accept when you are rebuilding and receiving younger players or draft picks that will appreciate in value over time. The key is to always have a clear reason for making the trade beyond just liking a particular player.

When to Reject a Trade

Reject a trade when the value gap is significant and not justified by roster needs. Reject when you are being asked to sell a cornerstone player for a package of marginal assets. Reject when the trade moves you laterally rather than forward, swapping one decent player for another without improving your team. Reject when the other manager is clearly trying to buy low on your player during a temporary slump. If you are unsure, run the numbers through Fantasy Draft Pros first. If the trade analyzer shows a large deficit on your side, trust the data and either reject or counter with a more balanced proposal.
